Firemaking is the skill used to light logs with a tinderbox, creating temporary fires that can also be used for Cooking. At the 7 September 2004 cutoff, the standard progression ran from normal logs at level 1 through Magic logs at level 75, with members also having Achey tree logs. Maple logs themselves are not members-only to burn: the period source trees were in members' areas, but build 254 does not mark the logs as members-only. Magic logs are the members-only standard tier.

How Firemaking works

Basic loop: use a tinderbox with a supported log in the inventory, or choose the ground log's Light option. The build checks the player's current Firemaking level against the log's requirement. An ignition attempt can fail and be retried; a successful attempt consumes the log, awards the configured XP, creates a temporary fire, and eventually leaves ashes.

  • Current level is checked: the standard handler reads the current Firemaking stat, not just the unboosted base value. The unlock table below still records the normal base-level requirements.
  • Lighting can take more than one attempt: qualifying for a log does not guarantee that the first ignition roll catches. The script repeats the lighting attempt until it succeeds or the action is interrupted.
  • Some tiles reject fires: the handler checks the map for blocked or restricted placements before allowing a fire to be lit, so not every standing tile is a valid Firemaking spot.
  • Fire duration is not a log-tier bonus: build 254 uses the same random duration range for the supported logs rather than making higher-level logs burn longer.
  • Movement after lighting: once a fire is created, the player is moved away from the occupied square when a neighbouring tile is available. The implementation has a preferred direction order and a special Duel Arena case.
  • Fires are utility objects: ordinary supported raw food can be cooked on a player-made fire. When the fire expires, it is replaced by ashes.

No invented XP/hour: build evidence gives exact XP per successful fire but does not by itself establish a period-correct banking route, player click rate, interruption rate, or hourly throughput. This guide therefore compares per-log XP and practical constraints rather than presenting unsupported XP/hour figures.

2004scape implementation notes

Standard log implementation: scripts/skill_firemaking/scripts/firemaking.rs2 performs the placement, level, ignition, XP, fire and ash behaviour. scripts/skill_firemaking/configs/firemaking.obj supplies the standard logs' level requirements, XP and membership flags. The fuller in-game Firemaking guide in scripts/player/configs/skill_guide.dbrow independently lists the normal, Oak, Willow, Maple, Yew and members-only Magic progression.

  • Maple correction: the old wiki page labelled Maple logs as members-only. The build's Maple-log object has no members=yes flag, and the skill-guide row simply says Maple logs; the corresponding Magic row explicitly says Members: Magic logs. The rewritten table therefore marks Maple burning as free-to-play.
  • XP scale: standard productexp values are stored at ten times the displayed skill XP. Normal through Magic are 400, 600, 900, 1350, 2025 and 3038 respectively, yielding 40, 60, 90, 135, 202.5 and 303.8 XP.
  • Achey discrepancy: scripts/skill_firemaking/scripts/achey.rs2 has a dedicated lighting handler that reads the object's levelrequire and productexp. The current achey_tree_logs object, however, supplies neither parameter. The relevant parameter defaults are zero, so the checkout appears to treat Achey logs as requiring no effective Firemaking level and awarding no Firemaking XP. Historical progression is level 1 / 40 XP, so this page records the historical value and flags the build mismatch instead of silently treating the current data as authoritative history.
  • Light-source distinction: light_source.rs2 lights configured candles/torches without using the standard log XP routine. The Sea Slug script separately applies its own 30-Firemaking dry-stick check.
  • Quest fire arrows: the Underground Pass handler converts prepared unlit arrows to lit arrows but neither checks Firemaking nor advances the skill.

Cutoff exclusions

Current checkout does not equal the historical snapshot: later content can exist in the source tree and still fall outside this wiki's 7 September 2004 target. The following systems are intentionally excluded from the period unlock/training tables.

  • Tai Bwo Wannai Trio / jogre-bone Firemaking: released 14 September 2004, one week after the cutoff.
  • Shades of Mort'ton funeral pyres: released 18 October 2004, after the cutoff.
  • Coloured firelighters and later Firemaking additions: these belong to later updates and are not presented as September 2004 features.
