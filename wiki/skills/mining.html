Mining extracts ore, essence and gems from rocks and supplies most raw material used by Smithing. At the 7 September 2004 cutoff, ordinary ore progression runs from Clay, Copper and Tin at level 1 through Iron, Coal, Gold, Mithril and Adamantite to Runite at level 85. Members also have quest-linked Blurite, Elemental and perfect-gold interactions plus Shilo Village gem rocks.

How Mining works

Basic loop: choose Mine on a rock while carrying or wielding a pickaxe your current Mining level can use. Build 254 checks the rock requirement, selects the best usable pickaxe in your inventory or weapon slot, repeatedly rolls for success, awards the rock's XP and item on success, and normally turns the rock into a depleted state until it respawns.

  • Current Mining level is used: build 254 reads the current stat both for rock requirements and for deciding which pickaxe is usable. Temporary boosts therefore affect actions whose checks are reached while the boost is active.
  • You do not have to wield the pickaxe: the pickaxe checker accepts a usable pickaxe in the weapon slot or ordinary inventory. It automatically prefers Rune, then Adamant, Mithril, Steel, Iron and Bronze when several usable pickaxes are available.
  • Better pickaxes make checks happen sooner: the build assigns a decreasing mining-rate value from Bronze 7 to Rune 2. These are timing parameters, not success percentages; the rock's own success roll is still level-dependent.
  • Most rocks deplete: ordinary ore and gem rocks become empty after a successful mine and later respawn. Build 254 scales rock respawn timing with player count, so raw configuration values should not be read as fixed real-world seconds.
  • Prospecting is separate: Prospect identifies the mineral in a live rock and reports an empty rock when it has been depleted. Rune Essence uses its own prospect text.
  • Special rocks can bypass the ordinary loop: Rune Essence does not deplete; Elemental Workshop's level-20 rock creates an Earth elemental rather than directly awarding ore; and Family Crest changes successful Gold ore into perfect gold inside the quest mine.

Pickaxes

Pickaxe level gates and the build's mining-rate parameters are straightforward. The rate number is an internal timing value: lower is faster. It is not a percentage chance to mine the ore.

Best-tool selection: carrying a Rune pickaxe does not help if your current Mining level is below 41. In that case the checker falls back through the lower tiers until it finds the best one you can currently use.

Gems and the Amulet of glory

Ordinary ore mining and Shilo Village gem rocks use two different gem systems in build 254.

Random gems while mining ordinary rocks

  • Before the ordinary ore-success roll, supported rocks perform a separate gem check. The normal build check is 1 in 256.
  • On a members map, wearing a charged Amulet of glory changes that check to 1 in 86. The uncharged Glory is not in the build's mining-boost category.
  • A successful pre-check then uses a second table that can yield Sapphire, Emerald, Ruby or Diamond — or no gem. This means the 1-in-256 / 1-in-86 figure is the trigger for the gem table, not a guarantee that every trigger produces a gem.
  • The build's ordinary-rock gem table does not include Dragonstone. A period community guide that mentions Dragonstone in this context is therefore not used as evidence for build behaviour.

Mining Guild

The Mining Guild beneath Falador is free-to-play and normally requires 60 Mining. Build 254 checks the player's current Mining stat at both the door and ladder, not a stored base level.

  • Dwarven stout boost: build 254's ordinary Dwarven stout raises Mining and Smithing by +1 while lowering Attack, Strength and Defence by 3. A player at base 59 Mining can therefore reach 60 and enter while the +1 Mining boost is active.
  • Period facilities: a July-2004 RuneScape 2 guide describes the guild as containing a large concentration of Coal with a smaller number of Mithril rocks; its period count is 37 Coal and 5 Mithril.
  • Access is re-checked: if your temporary boost has already fallen before you operate the entrance, the build rejects entry below current level 60.

2004scape implementation notes

Core implementation: scripts/skill_mining/scripts/mining.rs2 handles ordinary mining and special dispatch; pickaxe_checker.rs2 selects the best usable pickaxe; mine.dbrow supplies ordinary rock levels, XP and success parameters; and levelup_unlocks_mining.rs2 provides the familiar milestone messages.

  • Success formula caution: ordinary rocks call the engine's level-scaled stat_random with each rock's low/high parameters. Those database values are therefore recorded here as roll parameters rather than falsely converted into simple fixed percentages.
  • Iron XP conflict: build 254 awards 35 XP per Iron ore. The direct RS2 guide updated 14 July 2004 records 30 XP. The page exposes both values instead of using later familiarity to erase the conflict.
  • Blurite reconstruction TODO: the build's Blurite row correctly uses level 10 and 17.5 XP, but its success parameters are marked as a TODO/placeholder based on Limestone data. Those parameters are not presented as historically proven September-2004 Blurite rates.
  • Rune Essence special path: essence mining bypasses ordinary rock depletion/success handling and directly awards one essence plus 5 Mining XP on its cycle. Access to the mine itself is quest-gated by Rune Mysteries.
  • Perfect gold coordinate substitution: inside Family Crest's quest mine, the ordinary Gold-rock success path creates perfect_gold_ore instead of normal Gold ore while retaining Gold's Mining requirement and XP.
  • Elemental Workshop special path: its level-20 earth rock checks for a usable pickaxe, then spawns an Earth elemental. The elemental's death-drop handler supplies the Elemental ore; the rock interaction itself does not advance Mining XP.
  • Random mining events: the current build can interrupt Mining with gas, a lost pickaxe head, a rock golem and general random-event logic. The rock-golem script itself comments that one ranged attack was added in December 2006, so that later attack is treated as a build reconstruction deviation rather than September-2004 historical evidence.
  • Charged Glory implementation: only charged Glory variants are placed in the build category used by Mining. The ordinary-rock gem boost is also restricted to the members map.

Cutoff exclusions

Source-tree presence is not enough to make content September-2004 content. Release chronology takes priority when the current checkout contains later Mining additions.

  • Limestone: the current Mining database contains a level-10 Limestone row, but Limestone was introduced on 20 September 2004, after this wiki's 7 September cutoff. It is excluded from the historical progression table.
  • Sandstone and Granite: these desert Mining rocks were introduced with later 2006 content and are outside the target snapshot.
  • Black, Dragon and later pickaxes: build 254's audited Mining pickaxe table ends at Rune. Later tool tiers are not projected backward into the September-2004 guide.
  • Later mining areas and training methods: later-game quarry systems, expanded guild mechanics and modern optimisation routes are not substituted for the period evidence above.
