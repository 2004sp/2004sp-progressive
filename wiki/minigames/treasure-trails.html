Treasure Trails are a members-only chain of clue scrolls. A qualifying monster or other build-specific source gives an easy, medium or hard clue; solving it either advances directly to another clue or gives a casket that advances the trail when opened. Continue until the tier's completion roll succeeds, then the game generates several reward rolls from that tier's table. There is no ticket currency, points system or reward shop: the items listed below are generated directly by the reward scripts.

Historical/build split: Treasure Trails launched on 5 May 2004, and the launch announcement described clues as members-server NPC drops, stronger monsters tending to give harder clues, and only one active trail at a time. The historical cutoff for this wiki is 7 September 2004. The current game_trail source also contains clue and reward additions from the 26 October 2004 Treasure Trails expansion and god pages from 17 November 2004. Those later entries are labelled below and are not presented as September-2004 content.

Where Treasure Trails start

There is no minigame lobby, entrance gate or fixed starting NPC. In the target period, Treasure Trails begin when a members-server NPC/monster drops a clue. Build 254 implements that model with tier-specific clue-drop procedures and also has a later, post-cutoff Mort'ton catacomb reward path that can call the generic clue generators.

All three drop procedures first check that the player is on a members map and does not already have a clue/casket. If eligible, the procedure rolls the supplied denominator; on success it chooses a random entry from that tier's clue enum and clears the existing trail-progress bits.

Only one active trail

The original 5 May 2004 announcement explicitly says a player cannot be on more than one Treasure Trail at once. Build 254 enforces this more concretely than a modern one-per-tier system: trail_hasclue_all searches both the inventory and bank for any easy, medium or hard clue or casket. If any one is present, another clue drop is suppressed and ground-pickup code replies, “You already have a clue scroll.”

Challenge scrolls and puzzle boxes have their own duplicate checks, but the main trail gate is cross-tier. In practical build-254 terms, banking an easy clue still prevents a medium or hard clue from being generated.

How a Treasure Trail works

  1. Obtain one clue. The build chooses a random clue object from the tier enum and resets the trail step counter.
  2. Read it. Text clues open the short or long parchment interface. Map clues open a dedicated map interface.
  3. Solve the clue type. This can mean speaking to an NPC, searching/opening an object, obtaining a key, answering a challenge, solving an anagram, digging at a map/coordinate location, or completing a sliding puzzle.
  4. Advance the trail. A solved non-dig clue calls that tier's progress routine directly. Dig clues usually replace the clue with a casket; opening the casket then calls the same progress routine.
  5. Roll for completion. The progress counter increments first. Easy can finish after 2–4 solved steps, medium after 3–5, and hard after 4–6. At the first two eligible near-cap steps the build has a 50% finish roll; the maximum step is guaranteed.
  6. If not finished, receive another clue. The current clue/challenge/casket is removed and another random clue from the same tier enum is added.
  7. If finished, roll rewards. The tier reward script fills the temporary reward inventory, displays the Treasure Trail completion message/jingle and moves those items into the player's inventory.

Coordinate tools: sextant, watch and chart

You only need the navigation set when carrying a clue with the coordinate/sextant flag. In build 254 the helper dialogue is available only while such a clue is present.

  1. Speak to the Observatory Professor and choose the Treasure Trails dialogue. He explains coordinates and sends you to Murphy for a sextant and to the Clock Tower for a watch.
  2. Speak to Murphy at Port Khazard. He gives you a sextant.
  3. Speak to Brother Kojo at the Clock Tower south of Ardougne. He gives you a watch.
  4. Return to the Observatory Professor with both. He gives you the navigation chart.

The Professor's Treasure Trails dialogue is available before starting the Observatory Quest and again after completing it, but the build does not expose it during the quest's intermediate states. Once the navigation progression is complete, Murphy, Brother Kojo and the Professor can replace their respective lost tool if you no longer own it.

Using the sextant opens its own interface. To obtain a reading you must also carry the watch and chart, be above ground and outside disabled zones, then align the horizon and sun in the eyepiece. The output is measured relative to the Observatory in degrees and minutes north/south and east/west.

Dropping, abandoning and recovery

  • Historical rule: the launch announcement says dropping the current clue abandons the trail and allows NPCs to drop clues again.
  • Build-254 nuance: dropping a clue removes it from the inventory/bank check, so new clues can generate, but there is no dedicated drop handler that clears the step counter at the instant of dropping. The counter is cleared when a new clue is generated. Picking the same dropped clue back up before starting another trail can therefore preserve the existing build progress.
  • Picking up while holding another clue: blocked across all tiers with “You already have a clue scroll.”
  • Lost coordinate tools: after the navigation dialogue has progressed, Murphy, Brother Kojo and the Observatory Professor can replace missing sextant/watch/chart items.
  • Lost key/challenge/puzzle item: re-trigger the clue's relevant NPC/monster interaction where the script supports it. The main clue itself is still required to satisfy those clue-specific checks.
  • Death: Treasure Trails define no universal safe-death override. Hard clues can take you into the Wilderness or spawn combat guardians, so ordinary build-254 death/item-loss rules still matter.
  • Multiplayer: trail progress and guardian-defeated state are player variables. Spawned hard-clue wizards are assigned to the triggering player, while world NPCs/objects used for ordinary clue interactions remain shared entities.

Reward generation

Rewards are rolled once per tier completion, with multiple independent reward rolls. A roll first decides whether to use that tier's normal or rare table, then selects uniformly within the chosen table. Duplicate items can therefore occur and stack where the object is stackable.

No reward shop or points exchange

Treasure Trails have no ticket shop, points currency, exchange NPC, stock inventory, restock timer or XP-for-points option in build 254. The trail_reward interface only displays the random reward inventory created by the completion script. It must not be described as a shop. There is also no direct XP reward from completing a trail.

Build deviations and cutoff exclusions

  • 26 October clue expansion: the current clue enums contain appended easy, medium and hard clues released after the target date. They are source-visible but not September history.
  • 26 October reward expansion: decorated plateskirts and gilded rune armour are present in reward code but post-date the cutoff.
  • 17 November god pages: all three reward scripts can roll god pages through a shared helper, but Horror from the Deep and the pages were released after the cutoff.
  • Later acquisition call sites: Afflicted/Mort'ton, Rellekka/Fremennik, Horror-from-the-Deep and Regicide-era scripts call clue helpers in the current source tree. Those calls are build/source evidence only for this audit, not target-date historical instructions.
  • Drop-to-abandon state: historical launch text treats dropping the clue as abandonment; build 254 does not clear the progress bits until another clue is generated.
  • Reward odds reconstruction: comments in the reward scripts explicitly derive/scaled rare rates from later disclosed odds. The exact 1/84, 1/66 and 1/45 build denominators are therefore not asserted as original May/September server values.
  • Sextant reconstruction: the sextant script contains source comments acknowledging uncertainty around disabled zones and randomized needle positioning. The coordinate output and tool requirement are usable build mechanics, while those low-level details remain reconstruction notes.

Historical evidence and cutoff notes

  • 5 May 2004 official update: introduced Treasure Trails, described random NPC clue drops on members servers, harder clues from stronger monsters, chained clues leading to treasure and the one-active-trail rule.
  • 26 October 2004 official update: explicitly added more clues and new Treasure Trail rewards, making that expansion later than the 7 September snapshot.
  • Reward item histories: decorated plateskirts and gilded rune armour date to 26 October 2004, while god pages date to 17 November 2004. The older decorated platelegs/helms/bodies/shields and original clothing rewards remain period-valid.
  • Clue item histories: surviving clue records identify original medium anagrams such as ARE COL/GOBLIN KERN as 5 May 2004 items, while later appended coordinates are dated 26 October 2004. This matches the build enum/config boundary used in the audit.
  • Build-254 source: all 50 files under Content/scripts/minigames/game_trail/, plus the global spade handler and cross-module NPC/drop/progress call sites described above.
