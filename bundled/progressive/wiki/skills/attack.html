Attack is a free-to-play combat skill that determines melee accuracy and gates stronger melee weapons. Raising Attack makes successful melee hits more likely; it does not directly raise maximum melee damage, which is primarily governed by Strength and equipment strength bonuses. By 7 September 2004, the important equipment milestones were the ordinary metal tiers through rune, members-only spears and claws, Excalibur at 20 Attack, and several level-60 dragon weapons with different quest requirements.

Mechanics

Direct Attack training: choose an accurate melee style. A normal accurate hit awards 4 Attack XP per point of damage and gives an invisible +3 Attack-level modifier while that style is selected. A controlled style instead shares the combat XP between Attack, Strength and Defence, awarding roughly 1.33 XP in each skill per point of damage and giving an invisible +1 modifier to all three melee skills.

  • Accuracy: Attack level and the equipment's stab, slash or crush attack bonus contribute to whether a melee attack lands. The target's defensive statistics and bonuses oppose that roll.
  • Damage: Attack improves the chance of connecting, not the maximum hit. Training Strength is what primarily increases melee maximum damage.
  • Attack styles: the exact buttons depend on the weapon. Accurate styles train Attack directly; aggressive styles train Strength; defensive styles train Defence; controlled styles divide XP between the three.
  • Hitpoints XP: successful damaging melee hits also award Hitpoints experience independently of which melee combat style is selected.
  • Temporary combat boosts: Attack potions, super attack potions and Attack-boosting prayers can improve effective melee accuracy. This guide does not use temporary boosts to advertise equipment unlocks; each weapon row shows its normal required Attack level.
  • Danger: Attack can be trained almost anywhere melee combat is possible. Food, armour, monster aggression, Wilderness PvP and the target's combat strength affect safety, but they do not change the underlying Attack XP style rules.

Training methods

The core training choice in 2004 is the combat style, not a modern optimised monster route. Fight targets you can sustain safely, keep an appropriate weapon equipped, and choose accurate whenever Attack itself is the priority.

Other relevant quests and areas

  • Demon Slayer provides Silverlight, a free-to-play special weapon used to defeat Delrith.
  • Merlin's Crystal provides Excalibur. It requires 20 Attack to wield in build 254, although that quest can use the sword from the inventory for its crystal interaction.
  • Holy Grail makes the 20-Attack Excalibur equip requirement practically important because the Black Knight Titan must be defeated with the sword equipped in build 254.
  • Lost City is the quest gate for the level-60 Dragon dagger and Dragon longsword in build 254.
  • Heroes' Quest unlocks the Heroes' Guild and the level-60 Dragon mace and Dragon battleaxe progression.
  • Biohazard unlocks the Combat Training Camp, including the small 300-Attack-XP dummy sequence documented by the build.

No Warriors' Guild at this date: the Burthorpe Warriors' Guild and defenders were released in June 2006. They are therefore not an Attack-training or equipment feature for the September 2004 snapshot.

2004scape implementation notes

Earlier audited quest work in this repository established several build-254 equipment checks that matter here: Excalibur requires 20 Attack to equip; Lost City gates the Dragon dagger and Dragon longsword at 60 Attack; Heroes' Quest gates the Dragon mace and Dragon battleaxe; and Death Plateau unlocks claw smithing. The Attack page follows those already-recorded build findings while keeping historical cutoff facts separate.

The previous Attack guide also included metal and Dragon halberds. Those rows have been removed from target-period content because Regicide and the standard halberd release are dated 20 September 2004. Other later staples—the Warriors' Guild, defenders, Dragon scimitar, abyssal weapons and later combat-training systems—are likewise excluded.
