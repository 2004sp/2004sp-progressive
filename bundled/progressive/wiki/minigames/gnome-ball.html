Gnome Ball is a members-only sport on the pitch in the north-west of the Tree Gnome Stronghold. Talk to the Gnome ball referee for a ball, carry or pass it through the defending gnomes, then shoot at the eastern goal. Defenders can tackle you and steal possession; green Gnome wingers are friendly passing targets. Your personal score increases for each successful goal, and 5 goals wins a game.

Historical/build split: Gnome Ball was released on 28 January 2003 and was well inside this wiki's 7 September 2004 cutoff. A guide entered into RuneHQ's database on 11 March 2004 preserves a 4/5/6/7/30 XP progression in both Agility and Ranged for goals 1–5. Build 254 preserves the same Ranged progression, but its audited source awards Agility only on the fifth goal. This page therefore keeps the period reward table separate from the build-254 implementation table.

Getting there

The Gnome Ball pitch is in the north-west corner of the Tree Gnome Stronghold, west of the Grand Tree. The pitch entrance is on its western side beside the Gnome ball referee; the scoring goal is at the far eastern end.

  • From East Ardougne: travel north-west to the Tree Gnome Stronghold's main entrance, enter the Stronghold, then continue north-west through the settlement to the Gnome Ball pitch.
  • Spirit tree: after completing Tree Gnome Village, the period-valid spirit-tree network can take you to the Tree Gnome Stronghold. Walk west/north-west from the Stronghold tree to the pitch.
  • Gnome glider: after completing The Grand Tree, the glider network can bring you to the Grand Tree. From there, walk west to the Gnome Ball field.

Starting a game

  1. Open the gate and enter the pitch. Build 254 clears your hands for play and enables the player-to-player Pass interaction.
  2. Talk to the Gnome ball referee beside the western entrance. On the first conversation he explains that you use Ranged to throw at the net and can pass to the green wingers.
  3. Choose to play. The referee whistles, throws the Gnomeball into the air, and your character catches it in the right-hand slot.
  4. The score overlay appears in the upper-right corner and starts at 0.

If you miss a shot or otherwise lose a ball without a defender still carrying it, talk to the referee again for a new ball. If a defending gnome has stolen your ball, the referee considers that ball still in play; tackle the carrier to regain possession instead.

Passing

The two green Gnome wingers are on your side. Pass the ball to a winger, run downfield while the winger holds it, then catch the return pass closer to the goal. In build 254 the winger's return timer is randomised after receiving the ball, producing a short delay before the pass comes back.

  • To a winger: use the ball on a green Gnome winger. The ball leaves your hand, the winger changes to a ball-carrying state, then throws it back if you are still on the pitch with a free right hand.
  • To another player: build 254 also supports passing between players. Both players must be on the pitch, the receiver must have a free right hand, and the receiver cannot already have another defender-carried ball registered in play.
  • No passing XP: no stat_advance call exists in either passing path. Passing is tactical movement, not a direct XP action in build 254.

Tackling and possession

Yellow, orange and red Gnome ballers are defenders. Their hunt config targets a player carrying the Gnomeball when there is line of sight. When a defender reaches you, Agility is used in the tackle roll.

Shooting and scoring

The goal is at the eastern end of the field. You must be on the pitch, have the Gnomeball in your right hand, and stand in front of the goal. Use Shoot on the goal to throw the ball.

  • Ranged matters: build 254 uses your Ranged stat in the shot-success roll.
  • Distance matters: the same build roll also includes distance to the goal, so closer attempts are favoured.
  • Miss: the ball lands away from the goal, is removed from play, your score does not change, and no XP is awarded. Talk to the referee for another ball.
  • Goal: your score increases by 1, the cheerleaders call “GOAL”, and the shot awards XP according to the current score.
  • Fifth goal: the build displays “You win!”, marks you as owed a reward Gnomeball, awards the win XP, and immediately resets the score to 0 so another five-goal cycle can begin.

Score, rounds and leaving

Each player has a personal Gnomeball Score overlay. There is no fixed match timer and no opposing team score to beat: completing a game means reaching 5 successful goals.

  • After a win: the score resets from 5 to 0 immediately. You are not forced out; you can ask the referee for another play ball and continue.
  • Leaving normally: open the west gate. The activity resets your score, removes any in-game Gnomeball, and clears the pass interaction. If you are owed a reward ball, one Gnomeball is returned to you as you leave.
  • Leaving the map zone mid-game: build 254 calls you out of the game, deletes play balls from inventory/equipment and resets the activity state.
  • Dropping a play ball: trying to drop the Gnomeball while in the pitch deletes it with a message saying it magically returns to the referee. Talk to him for a replacement.
  • Logging in inside the pitch: the login procedure teleports the player to the entrance area and clears the active score/possession state. The source contains special reconstruction notes around whether an equipped owed ball survives this path.

Death and item-loss rules

Gnome Ball is not implemented as a protected safe-death minigame. Successful defender tackles use the ordinary player-damage queue, and the audited Gnome Ball folder contains no special death override that protects carried items. If tackle damage kills a player, normal build-254 death/item-loss handling applies. The referee dialogue itself warns that humans have died on the field, so low-Hitpoints players should bring food.

Gnomeball reward and repeatability

Winning a five-goal game sets gnomeball_owedball. The reward is actually handed over when you leave the pitch: if there is inventory space the build adds one Gnomeball to the inventory; if the inventory is full it equips the ball in the right hand as part of the exit path.

  • The reward is repeatable: complete another five-goal cycle to become owed another ball for that visit.
  • There is no cooldown or daily limit in the audited source.
  • However, the owed-ball state is a single boolean, not a counter. Multiple wins before leaving do not accumulate multiple owed reward balls in build 254.
  • If you enter while already carrying one or more Gnomeballs, the gate removes them for “safe keeping” and sets the same single owed-ball flag. Because the flag is only boolean, bringing multiple balls in can collapse them to a single returned ball on exit.

Reward shop / exchange

No Gnome Ball ticket, point or reward shop exists. The audited folder has no ticket currency, exchange NPC, reward-shop inventory or shop interface. Rewards are direct XP from successful goals plus the completion Gnomeball. There are no coin rewards, random reward tables, stock limits or purchase requirements.

Multiplayer behaviour and shared-state limitations

Gnome Ball supports multiple players on the field, including direct player-to-player passing. Each player's score and game progress are personal vars, but the ballers and wingers are shared world NPCs.

  • Personal score: one player's goal does not increment another player's score.
  • Player passing: two active players can throw the Gnomeball directly between their right-hand slots.
  • Winger contention: a winger tracks one attacking player UID and has a busy window, so concurrent users can temporarily block one another from using that winger.
  • Defender possession is not fully isolated: a ball-carrying defender is a shared NPC state. The carrier-tackle path does not verify that the tackling player is the UID for whom that defender originally stole the ball, so another player can potentially recover it.
  • No team score: multiplayer is cooperative/interactive possession play, not a formal player team versus a scored gnome team.

Build deviations, bugs and reconstruction notes

  • Agility XP mismatch: build 254 omits Agility XP on goals 1–4 compared with the March-2004-origin reward table.
  • Single owed-ball flag: reward ownership is boolean rather than counted, so multiple wins before leaving do not accumulate multiple balls and multiple brought-in balls can collapse to one return.
  • Tackle odds reconstructed: comments in gnome_baller.rs2 label tackle-roll tuning as a guess. Agility involvement is authoritative; exact historical percentages are not.
  • Shared defender possession: a different player can potentially tackle a shared ball-carrying defender and take a ball associated with someone else's play state.
  • Shared winger state: winger ownership/busy timing is NPC-scoped, so simultaneous players can contend for the same passing NPC.
  • Login edge cases: the login recovery source contains explicit test notes about equipped and smuggled Gnomeballs, showing that this path is reconstructed around observed edge behaviour rather than a clean isolated session model.

Historical evidence and cutoff notes

  • Jagex, “Gnomeball” (28 January 2003), preserved by the RuneScape Classic Wiki: establishes the release date, Gnome Stronghold setting and the deliberately rough tackling premise.
  • RuneHQ Gnome Ball guide, database entry dated 11 March 2004: preserves the north-west Stronghold location, referee start, winger passing, five-goal win, Gnomeball completion reward and 4/5/6/7/30 Agility + Ranged XP table used for the cutoff-period comparison.
  • RuneScape/Old School historical reference pages: corroborate members-only status, no skill requirement, the west-of-Grand-Tree pitch, green wingers, the five-point Gnomeball reward and the use of Ranged/Agility.
  • Build-254 source: all scripts/configs/interfaces under Content/scripts/minigames/game_gnomeball/, plus the Gnome Ball checks in Content/scripts/player/scripts/equip.rs2 and the relevant varp definitions.

Post-cutoff exclusions: Gnome Restaurant did not arrive until 2006, so its later Gnomeball reward source is not part of this September-2004 activity guide. Modern achievements, Surge, later interface changes and other post-2004 convenience mechanics are likewise excluded from the historical instructions.
