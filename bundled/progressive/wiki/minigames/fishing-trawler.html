Fishing Trawler is a cooperative members minigame run by Murphy at Port Khazard. Board the trawler, keep its leaking hull under control with swamp paste, bail accumulated water with a bailing bucket, and repair a ripped net with rope. If the boat remains afloat for the full trip, Murphy returns to Port Khazard and each player can inspect the landed net for a level-dependent catch. Build 254 has no contribution meter, no points currency and no reward shop.

Historical/build split: Fishing Trawler was released on 28 July 2003 and therefore predates the 7 September 2004 cutoff. A RuneScape FAQ updated on 14 July 2004 records the activity north of Yanille, the level-15 minimum, and the level-79/81 Sea turtle and Manta ray rewards at 105/115 Fishing XP. Build 254 reconstructs the same old-style repair-and-bail activity, but some internal probability formulas are explicitly guesses and some intermediate reward thresholds differ from period ordinary-Fishing tables. Those deviations are labelled below rather than silently treated as historical fact.

Getting there

The trawler is moored at Port Khazard on Kandarin's east coast, north-east of Yanille, south of East Ardougne and immediately east of the Khazard Fight Arena. Murphy stands by the docks beside the boat.

  • From East Ardougne: leave the city to the south and follow the coast/road south toward the Fight Arena and Port Khazard. This is a straightforward period-valid walking route.
  • From Yanille: leave Yanille to the north-east and walk toward the coast, then continue north to Port Khazard.
  • From the Fight Arena: walk directly east to the coast; the Port Khazard docks and Murphy are beside the trawler.

Getting the supplies

  • Port Khazard general store: the build shop near the docks stocks rope, pots of flour, Bailing buckets and Swamp paste. Murphy's own dialogue tells the player to get supplies from the shop at the end of the pier.
  • Making swamp paste: Murphy describes the period recipe as swamp tar mixed with flour, then heated over a fire. His dialogue points to the swamps below Lumbridge as the tar source.
  • Recommended rather than required: there is no script that checks for a fixed amount of rope, paste or buckets before boarding. Bring enough for the whole team; a trip can technically begin with none and then fail quickly.

Starting a trip

  1. Talk to Murphy for the period instructions. He explains the level-15 catch minimum, ripped nets, rope, Crafting difficulty and swamp paste.
  2. Cross the gangplank to board the waiting trawler. Build 254 does not actually require the Murphy dialogue flag before boarding, so speaking to him is instructional rather than a hard entry gate.
  3. When the first player boards and no trip is already active, the lobby schedules departure after 100 game ticks, about one minute. Other players can board during that window.
  4. The sailing cutscene moves the boarded group to the active trawler and opens the old How to play panel showing rope, swamp paste and bailing instructions.
  5. After the departure setup finishes, Murphy's five-tick control timer starts the ten-minute active trip.

If another trip is already in progress, the gangplank reports that someone is already on a fishing trip. The build keeps the dock/lobby separate from the active ship state and does not start a second simultaneous trip.

Flooding, sinking and the timer

The authoritative trip controller runs every 5 game ticks. Its trip counter ends successfully at 200 cycles = 1,000 ticks, which is about 10 minutes of active trawling. Murphy announces remaining minutes every 20 cycles. Preserved Trawler documentation likewise records a ten-minute successful trip; later interfaces that displayed other times are outside this target.

  • Leak scaling: the build allows up to min(players × 3 + 1, 9) simultaneous leaks. The leak-spawn chance also scales with player count, but the source labels the exact probability as a reconstruction guess.
  • Flood transition: when the flooding state reaches 30, players are shifted to the visibly flooded version of the ship.
  • Recovery: fixing leaks stops their repeated flooding gain; bailing actively subtracts water. If flooding drops below 1, the build moves players back to the non-flooded ship state.
  • Failure: at 100 flooding, the controller sinks the trawler and moves all players on the active ship to the wreck state.
  • Logout: trawler_login teleports a player who logs back in inside an active-game or wreck zone to the shore escape point rather than restoring them to the trip.

Catch accumulation and multiplayer behaviour

Fishing Trawler's build reward system is cooperative but not contribution-based. While the net is intact, every five-tick control cycle performs one 1-in-10 catch-progress roll for each player currently counted on the trip. Each success increments one shared catch counter. At the successful end, the build divides that shared total by the current player count using integer division and assigns the resulting number of reward rolls to every player.

  • No personal contribution score: patching more holes, bailing more water or repairing the net does not increase an individual player's share. There is no activity bar in build 254.
  • Net state matters to everyone: when the net is ripped, the shared catch counter stops increasing until someone repairs it.
  • Expected full-trip scale: with an intact net for all 200 cycles, the 1-in-10 per-player progress roll averages about 20 shared successes per player before the final equal division. This is an expectation, not a guaranteed catch size.
  • Player-count edge case: the divisor is taken from players present when the control timer completes. Leaving/logging out can therefore change the final division for the remaining group; the source has no separate locked-in crew list.

Success, failure, leaving and recovery

  • Successful trip: survive the full 200 control cycles. Murphy turns the boat toward shore, the return cutscene plays and each player is teleported back beside the docked catch net.
  • Zero catch: a trip can survive but accumulate zero reward rolls if the net stayed ripped or random catch progress was exceptionally poor. In that case the landed net cannot give you items.
  • Sunk trip: there is no catch payout. Players appear swimming around the wreck and must climb onto a reachable floating barrel to return to shore.
  • Escape damage: build 254 deals 2 damage after the barrel escape and says the player reaches shore tired and weary. The Trawler module does not define a special death-protection rule; it simply performs the escape and damage sequence.
  • Asking Murphy to return early: at sea, Murphy offers an “I've had enough” conversation. If the player insists, the build queues that player into the wreck/sinking state. It does not implement a clean personal return-without-failure path.
  • Implementation oddity: the early-return dialogue only queues the insisting player rather than calling the global sink procedure, so other crew members can remain on the active trip. Treat this as a build-specific multiplayer quirk, not historical instructions.

Claiming the catch

  1. After a successful return, inspect the Trawler net on the dock.
  2. The build checks your current Fishing level. Below 15, it refuses the claim but leaves the saved catch state intact.
  3. At 15+, the game opens the 40-slot Trawler Catch reward inventory and generates one reward item for each roll assigned at trip completion.
  4. Withdraw items individually or use the all-withdraw option. Closing/Dump Items causes any remaining reward slots to be dropped on the ground for a shortened loot duration.
  5. After the reward list is generated, your saved catch-roll count is cleared and the trip state is changed back to the sailed state so the same catch cannot be generated twice.

Boost-sensitive rewards: because the build reads stat(fishing) at the landed net, not when the voyage begins, a temporary Fishing boost used before claiming can unlock a higher reward branch. This behaviour is also consistent with preserved later Trawler documentation, but the exact build thresholds below are authoritative for build 254.

Exact total reward probabilities are level-dependent and should not be replaced with modern OSRS rates.

No reward shop or points exchange

Fishing Trawler has no tickets, points, reward currency, exchange NPC, stock-based prize shop, XP-for-points option or purchase cooldown. Rewards come directly from the landed Trawler net after a successful trip. The nearby Port Khazard general store sells activity supplies for coins, but it is an ordinary supply shop and not a minigame reward exchange.

Build deviations and reconstruction notes

  • No contribution system: later RuneScape/OSRS Trawler versions added anti-leech activity/contribution mechanics. Build 254 has none and must not be documented as requiring a minimum personal contribution.
  • Swordfish threshold: the build's Trawler reward path requires level 60 for Swordfish, while the July-2004 Fishing table records level 50 for ordinary Swordfish. The guide preserves the actual build gate and flags the mismatch.
  • Junk XP: the source comment says preserved old material gave 1 XP for junk except 2 for boots; the implementation deliberately guesses 1.2 XP for every junk result.
  • Leak probabilities: trawler_control.rs2 explicitly marks its leak chance as a “complete guess”. The leak/flood state machine is build fact; the exact probability is reconstruction.
  • Net repair probability: the Crafting check is also source-labelled mostly guessed from later observations. The one-rope cost on success and 5 Crafting XP are direct build behaviour.
  • Current-level reward roll: all fish quality is generated when the landed net is inspected. This makes boosts apply at claim time and means a sub-15 successful player can leave the saved catch unclaimed until reaching/boosting to 15.
  • Early-abandon multiplayer bug: insisting that Murphy return queues only the speaking player into the wreck state rather than terminating the shared trip.
  • Port Khazard shop quantities: the current Content config is annotated from September-2005 surviving stock evidence. The presence of the classic Trawler supplies is useful build evidence, but exact stock counts/restock timing are not projected back to 7 September 2004.

Post-cutoff content to exclude

  • Activity/contribution bars and anti-leech thresholds: later additions; absent from build 254.
  • Angler outfit / Trawler outfit rewards: later content; not in the build reward script.
  • Kraken events, broken rails and related contribution actions: later Trawler mechanics; absent here.
  • Ardougne Diary catch bonuses, pets and modern reward multipliers: later systems; not part of the September-2004 activity.
  • Tiger sharks, shark teeth and shell bonus items: later RuneScape Trawler rewards; none are reachable from build 254's reward path.

Historical evidence and cutoff notes

  • Release history: Fishing Trawler, Murphy and its wreck/escape scenery date to 28 July 2003, placing the activity well before the 7 September 2004 cutoff.
  • 14 July 2004 RuneScape FAQ: locates the Trawler north of Yanille/south of Ardougne, recommends a team, records level 15 as the minimum to fish on the trip, and records Trawler Sea turtles at 79/105 XP and Manta rays at 81/115 XP.
  • Preserved Trawler guides: retain the one-minute boarding window, ten-minute trip, swamp-paste leak repair, rope net repair, bailing-bucket recovery, wreck/barrel escape and landed-net reward flow. Later additions on those pages are not used as target-period evidence.
  • Build-254 source: all 21 files under Content/scripts/minigames/game_trawler/, plus the Trawler varps/reward inventory, shared randomjunk helper and Port Khazard supply-shop config.
