Lord Sinclair, a prominent Kandarin nobleman, has been murdered in his own study and the guards have no idea who did it. The killer is one of Lord Sinclair's six children, but which child is guilty is random. To solve the case you must make three independent pieces of evidence agree: thread caught on the study window, fingerprints lifted from the dagger, and a false story about how the murderer used a bottle of poison.

Requirements

  • Members' world.
  • No prerequisite quests, Quest Point requirement or skill levels.
  • No combat is required. The entire quest is an investigation.

Items and preparation

Bring

  • 1 empty pot. The kitchen flour barrel fills it with flour. Dusting an evidence item uses the flour but gives the empty pot back, so one pot is enough for the whole quest.
  • Several free inventory spaces. About 6–8 is comfortable if you follow the efficient route below.

Obtained during the quest

  • Criminal's dagger — required for the murderer's fingerprint.
  • Criminal's thread — required physical evidence and the first way to narrow the suspects.
  • Pungent pot — useful evidence that smells of the poison, but build 254 does not require this item for the final completion check.
  • Silver evidence belonging to the suspects — necklace, cup, bottle, book, needle or pot, depending on the child.
  • Flypaper — take at least three pieces if you use the thread to test only the two matching suspects.
  • Flour — taken from the kitchen barrel using your empty pot.
Do not copy a fixed culprit from another guide. The murderer is selected when you start the quest. Follow the evidence on your own account.

How the evidence works

The quest gives you three ways to identify the same person. The fastest route is to use the thread first, which leaves only two possible children, then fingerprint those two possessions and the dagger.

Detailed walkthrough

1. Start the investigation

  1. Travel to Sinclair Mansion north of Seers' Village.
  2. Speak to any Guard on the property and ask what is happening.
  3. Lord Sinclair has been found murdered and the guards are completely stuck. Agree to help investigate.
  4. The guard explains that Mary the maid found Lord Sinclair in the study on the eastern side of the ground floor. The room appeared to have been locked from the inside, there was a dagger, and there was also a strange smell.

2. Examine the study

  1. Enter the mansion and go to the eastern ground-floor study.
  2. Pick up the Criminal's dagger. It looks too flimsy to be a convincing murder weapon, which is an early hint that it may have been planted.
  3. Pick up the Pungent pot. Lord Sinclair appears to have been drinking from it before he died, and it carries the unusual smell noted by the guard.
  4. Investigate the damaged study window. A loose nail has caught a piece of coloured Criminal's thread. Take it.
  5. If you later lose the thread while the quest is active, investigate the window again. Build 254 can issue another piece after warning you to be more careful.

3. Rule out an outside intruder

  1. Go outside to the sturdy gate beside the Sinclair guard dog on the east side of the property.
  2. Investigate the gate. The dog barks loudly as you approach.
  3. This supports the intended deduction: an unknown intruder should have caused the dog to bark on the night of the murder. The killer was therefore probably somebody the dog already knew.

4. Use the thread to choose two suspects

  1. Check the colour of your Criminal's thread and use the table above to identify the two Sinclair children whose clothing matches it.
  2. Search the barrel in each of those two children's bedrooms and take their silver possession:
  • Anna: silver necklace.
  • Bob: silver cup.
  • Carol: silver bottle.
  • David: silver book.
  • Elizabeth: silver needle.
  • Frank: silver pot.

You can collect all six items if you want, but it is unnecessary. The thread already narrows the fingerprint test to two people.

5. Lift the fingerprints

  1. Go to the gardener's shed on the west side of the mansion grounds and investigate the sacks. Take three pieces of Flypaper: one for the dagger and one for each of your two suspects.
  2. Go to the mansion kitchen. Use your empty pot on the flour barrel to obtain a pot of flour.
  3. Use the pot of flour on the Criminal's dagger, then use Flypaper on the flour-covered dagger. This creates an Unknown print.
  4. Refill the same pot from the flour barrel. Dust the first suspect's silver item, then use Flypaper on it to make that child's fingerprint.
  5. Repeat for the second suspect's silver item.
  6. Use each suspect print on the Unknown print. The wrong print is rejected and destroyed. The matching print identifies the murderer and turns the dagger evidence into a Killer's print.

6. Establish the poison connection

  1. The Gossip near the mansion can point you toward a travelling poison seller. His conversation is a useful intended clue, although build 254 does not require it as a separate quest-state flag.
  2. Travel to Seers' Village and speak to the Poison Salesman.
  3. Ask who he sold poison to at the Sinclair house. He says that all six children — Anna, Bob, Carol, David, Elizabeth and Frank — bought a bottle of his multipurpose poison.
  4. If you brought the Pungent pot, you may show it to him. He recognises the smell as his poison and notes that the product is extremely poisonous if taken internally.

7. Prove the murderer's poison story is false

  1. Return to the mansion and talk to the child whose fingerprint matched the dagger.
  2. Ask why they bought poison. Every child has a different explanation.
  3. Investigate the place they claim to have poisoned. For the actual murderer, the place will show no sign that poison was used there, proving that their bottle was available for another purpose.

If you question the wrong child, their stated target will show evidence that poison really was used there. Return to the fingerprint result rather than guessing.

8. Present the complete case

  1. Return to a Guard and say that you know who did it.
  2. Once the three required evidence branches are complete, choose the option stating that you have conclusive proof.
  3. You explain that the guard dog rules out a stranger and the lack of a struggle makes the flimsy dagger look like misdirection.
  4. The thread matches the murderer's clothing, their supposed poison use is demonstrably false, and their fingerprints match those recovered from the dagger.
  5. The Guard accepts the case, takes the evidence and places the murderer under house arrest until trial. The quest completes.

Rewards

  • 3 Quest Points.
  • 1,406 Crafting XP.
  • 2,000 coins.

Useful recovery notes

  • Lost the Criminal's thread: investigate the study window again while the quest is active.
  • Need more Flypaper: the gardener's shed sacks can supply more.
  • Need more flour: refill the same empty pot at the kitchen flour barrel. Dusting an object returns the pot.
  • Wrong fingerprint destroyed: re-search that child's barrel for the silver object, dust it again and make another print if necessary.
  • The Guard will not accept the accusation: you need all three tracked evidence branches — thread, a matching dagger fingerprint, and proof that the identified murderer's poison alibi is false.
  • You do not need to fight anybody or obtain a special weapon. The dagger is evidence, not combat equipment for the quest.

2004scape implementation notes

  • Random murderer: starting the quest sets the murderer to one of six values corresponding to Anna, Bob, Carol, David, Elizabeth or Frank. This is why a fixed-answer walkthrough is wrong.
  • Thread mapping: build 254 gives green thread for Anna/David, red for Bob/Carol and blue for Elizabeth/Frank.
  • Fingerprint system: flour is consumed when dusting an object and the empty pot is returned. Flypaper is then consumed to lift a print. Comparing the correct child's print with the dagger's Unknown print sets the fingerprint-evidence flag; a wrong suspect print is destroyed.
  • Poison proof: the Poison Salesman must first reveal that all six children bought poison. Asking the actual murderer about their purchase advances the investigation, and inspecting that child's claimed target proves no poison was used there.
  • Completion gate: the Guard's conclusive-proof branch checks exactly three conditions: thread evidence, matching fingerprints and the completed poison-alibi investigation. The dog-gate deduction and Pungent pot are useful story evidence but are not separate coded completion gates.
  • Evidence cleanup: on completion, build 254 removes the quest evidence objects from the player's inventory and bank; it also handles a worn Anna necklace.
  • Reward: the completion script advances Crafting by 1,406 XP, adds 2,000 coins, and the global quest constant awards 3 Quest Points.
  • Flour after completion: the build's Sinclair Mansion flour barrel remains usable once the quest is complete because its access check only blocks players who have not yet started the quest. This is recorded as build behaviour rather than used to inflate the historical reward list.
  • Source reconstruction: several build dialogue/journal comments cite later videos, screenshots or RuneScape Classic material. The player-facing mechanics above were therefore cross-checked against the official December-2003 quest manual and the surviving RuneHQ guide whose database entry dates to 9 May 2004, rather than treating every reconstructed line of dialogue as direct September-2004 evidence.
